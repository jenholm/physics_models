\section{Perturbation analysis: failed completions as evidence}
\label{sec:perturbations}

The distance branch is background-only by construction: its fitted
object is an expansion history, with no perturbation dynamics attached
to it. A natural objection is that some perturbation completion of
that background might recover the CMB, collapsing the separation of
Section~\ref{sec:separation} into a single model. This section reports
why that route was tested, what was found, and why Model~B2 is
therefore treated as a comparison cosmology rather than a patched
distance model.

First, the audit of the distance-branch theory found no perturbation
state to extend: its fitted functions are interpolation coefficients
in background fit parameters, with no parent dynamical law from which
a perturbation of the modification could be derived. Candidate
derivations (direct variation, symmetry arguments, effective-field
routes, lapse reinterpretations) each failed to produce an admissible
extension, and the homogeneous-ansatz route was refused as an
unconstrained knob. The only admissible completion of that background
is therefore the one already tested: GR perturbations on the fitted
expansion history, which is exactly the branch~A evaluation reported
in Table~\ref{tab:ab-comparison}.

Second, perturbation freedom was tested where it \textit{is} well
defined: coupling rescaling around GR, implemented as a live modified-gravity
designer path in the Boltzmann solver with verified channel activation
(Appendix~F). Scanned over amplitude and transition epoch, including
directly on the distance-branch background, coupling rescaling never
produced finite low-$\ell$ TT and improved high-$\ell$ by at most a few
percent against a $\sim$$10^{4}$ deficit. The same freedom scanned
around the B2 background (Layer~3) likewise preferred GR. Perturbation
freedom of this form therefore cannot generate a viable completion of
the distance branch, and it adds nothing to the CMB branch.

These are not failures to hide; they are the evidence that fixes the
interpretation. Had any tested completion succeeded, the honest result
would have been one model, not two. What the tests support instead is
structural: completing the distance branch requires new perturbation
dynamics beyond anything tested here, while the CMB branch needs none.
Model~B2 accordingly stands beside Model~A as an independently
calibrated cosmology, and the measured distance between them is the
result.
